NAPS2 drives your scanner, gathers the pages in one window and saves them as a PDF, TIFF, JPEG or PNG. You scan from a flatbed or a document feeder, duplex included, with resolution, page size and bit depth set in a profile.

Before you save, you can fix the pages:

- drag them into a new order, or interleave two passes for manual duplex
- rotate them by hand, or deskew them automatically
- crop them, or change brightness and contrast
- run OCR to make the text in a PDF searchable

OCR uses Tesseract. It covers more than 100 languages, and the first time you choose Tools > OCR on a Mac, NAPS2 asks which of them to download and set up.

Scanners attached to another computer on your local network can be shared and used from your Mac. A command-line tool runs scans from scripts, and the interface is translated into over 40 languages. The project describes NAPS2 as free for people and businesses alike, with no ads and nothing bundled.

Version 8.6.0 needs macOS 12. It comes as an installer package for Apple silicon, for Intel or for both, and the universal package is notarized. On macOS 10.15 or 11, NAPS2 7.5.3 is the version to use.
