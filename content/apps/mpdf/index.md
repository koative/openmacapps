mPDF opens a PDF in a window that takes the shape of the page, so the page fills it from edge to edge. The toolbar stays out of the way. It slides down from the top when you move the pointer there.

A pages panel with thumbnails and the outline slides over the document. You drop PDF files or images into it to insert them between pages, or drop an image onto a page to place it. Pages can be reordered, duplicated, rotated or deleted, and blank pages added.

For filling in templates, the app handles form fields and adds:

- text boxes and check marks;
- images, shapes, arrows and freehand drawing;
- notes, highlights and white-out to hide what is underneath.

Everything you add can be moved, resized and undone. Search is built in. So are full screen, a presentation mode and a bookmark that reopens each file where you left it. The app is built on Apple's PDFKit and draws on ideas from Skim, without being affiliated with it. Releases are universal builds for macOS 14 or later, notarized, with the interface in English, French, Spanish and Italian and a daily update check.
