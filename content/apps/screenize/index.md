Screenize records a screen or a single window and then edits the recording for you. While you walk through a demo or tutorial, it logs the pointer, clicks and keystrokes. When you open the editor, zooms and pans that follow that activity are already on a multi-track timeline, and you can change any keyframe.

- Two camera modes: one follows the cursor live, the other plans zoom levels for typing, clicking, scrolling and dragging.
- Click ripples and on-screen badges for keyboard shortcuts.
- Gradient backgrounds, rounded corners, shadows, custom cursors and motion blur.
- Export to MP4, ProRes MOV or GIF at up to 4K and 240 fps, in sRGB, Display P3, BT.709 or BT.2020.

It captures system audio and the microphone and asks for four permissions: Screen Recording, Microphone, Input Monitoring and Accessibility. The interface comes in six languages, among them Korean, Japanese and German.

The author paused development in May 2026, writing that newer open-source recorders had raised the bar, and may return if those turn commercial-only. The 0.4.3 DMG on GitHub holds a Universal app for macOS 13 or later. It is not notarized, so the first launch needs a right-click and Open.

Screen Studio also zooms in on where you clicked. It adds camera layouts such as split screen and a camera overlay, and it is a paid app with license keys and subscriptions. Screenize is free under Apache 2.0.
