JabRef keeps your references in a BibTeX or BibLaTeX library and helps you fill it, sort it and cite from it while you write. The library is a plain text file, so you can put it under version control or share it through a synced folder.

Entries can come in from outside instead of being typed. JabRef searches online catalogues such as CrossRef, Google Scholar, IEEEXplore, PubMed, arXiv and zbMATH, fetches full records from an ISBN, DOI, PubMed ID or arXiv ID, extracts metadata from PDFs and imports more than 15 reference formats. A browser extension for Firefox, Chrome, Edge and Vivaldi sends a reference to JabRef in one click.

- Hierarchical groups built from keywords, search terms or manual assignment.
- Duplicate detection and merging, plus checks against curated catalogues to complete entries.
- A configurable citation key generator and custom fields and entry types.
- Linked PDFs and other files, renamed and moved according to rules you set.
- Reading status fields such as priority, ranking and printed.

For citing, JabRef pushes keys into editors including Emacs, LyX, Texmaker, TeXstudio and Vim, and inserts formatted citations into Word and LibreOffice using built-in or custom citation styles. A team can share one library through an SQL database. A command-line tool, JabKit, is also provided.

JabRef is free and runs on macOS, Windows and Linux. Version 5.15 ships for the Mac as DMG and PKG installers, with separate builds marked arm64 for Apple silicon.
