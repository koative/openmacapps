Quicksilver works in panes that you drive from the keyboard. Type a few letters to pick an app, file or bookmark in the first pane, choose what to do with it in the second, and fill a third pane when the action needs another object, such as a destination.

Actions go well past launching. You can move, copy, compress or email files, send messages and control music. Quicksilver learns your habits and adjusts what it shows first. Triggers attach a keyboard shortcut or mouse gesture to any action, so a command you repeat needs no typing at all. What it can find comes from catalog entries, listed under Preferences → Catalog, where a Custom section holds your own.

Hundreds of plugins connect it to other apps.

- Mail sends messages through Apple Mail or Gmail.
- A calculator copies its result to the clipboard.
- Clipboard and Shelf keep copy history and items you park for later.
- Browser plugins control Safari, Chrome and Firefox and search their bookmarks and history.
- Others create Calendar events and reminders, insert emoji, or resize, crop and convert images.

More than ten interface styles, with color themes, change how the panes look.

Version 2.6.0 needs macOS 10.14 or later. Its DMG holds a Universal app for Apple silicon and Intel, Developer ID-signed and notarized.

Alfred also launches apps and finds files from the keyboard. Its workflows, which chain hotkeys, keywords and actions, come with the Powerpack it sells separately. Quicksilver is free and open source under the Apache 2.0 license.
