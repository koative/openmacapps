Petrichor plays the music files you already own. Add your music folders and it reads their tags into an organised library, with a folder view when you want to browse by location instead.

- It imports 46 file extensions, from MP3, AAC and FLAC to APE, WavPack, DSD and Opus, and reads the real encoding so the Lossless badge is accurate.
- Smart playlists take conditional, negative and regular-expression rules, and ordinary playlists can be imported or exported.
- The play queue is rearranged by drag and drop, and almost anything can be pinned to the sidebar.
- Playback covers gapless tracks, crossfade, ReplayGain, an equalizer, Spatial Audio and AirPlay 2.
- Synced lyrics show when available, and missing ones can be downloaded.

Saved internet radio stations sit in collections next to the library. Controls appear in the menu bar and Dock, and Shortcuts and Siri can drive playback. For a smaller window there is a miniplayer, and an immersive mode fills the screen.

Most features lean on good metadata, so tidy tags pay off. DRM-protected purchases, audiobook files, MIDI and tracker modules do not play; MOD, IT, S3M and XM support ended with 1.7.

The app is sandboxed and never changes your files or folders. The developer says it collects no analytics and keeps library data offline. Lyrics, artist images, radio and Last.fm scrobbling each use the network and are off until you enable them.

Version 1.7.3 is a Universal DMG for Apple silicon and Intel, notarized, and needs macOS 14.
