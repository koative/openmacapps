Kodi turns a Mac into a home-theatre front end. You point it at your media, and it scans it into a personal library with box covers, descriptions and fan art. From there you browse and play from the couch, since the interface is built to be driven with a remote control.

It plays nearly all common audio and video formats and was designed with network playback in mind, so media can be streamed from elsewhere in the house or straight from the internet. Playlists, slideshows, a weather forecast and audio visualisations come with it. The look is set by a skinning engine, and add-ons, mostly written in Python and XML, extend and customise the rest.

The project dates from 2003 and is run by the non-profit XBMC Foundation with volunteer developers. The interface is translated into more than 70 languages, and the same app runs on Windows, Linux, BSD, Android, iOS and tvOS.

Team Kodi publishes separate builds for Apple silicon and Intel. The 21.3 Apple silicon DMG is notarized and needs macOS 11 or later.

Plex organises your files in Plex Media Server and streams them to Plex apps on your other devices, so the library and the player live on different machines. Infuse is a player for iPhone, iPad, Apple TV, Mac and Apple Vision. Kodi keeps the library and the player together on the machine it runs on, and that machine need not be an Apple one.
