Pixelorama is for drawing pixel art and animating it: sprites, tiles, game assets. You draw on a timeline of layers and frames, with onion skinning, frame tags, audio sync, and the option to keep drawing while the animation plays.

Tools map separately to the left and right mouse buttons, so two can be ready at once. Other parts are built around pixels:

- Pixel-perfect lines, an indexed color mode, and rotation and scaling algorithms such as cleanEdge, OmniScale and rotxel.
- Tilemap layers for rectangular, isometric or hexagonal tiles.
- Clipping masks and non-destructive layer effects, including outlines, gradient maps and drop shadows.
- Guides, rectangular and isometric grids, and a tile mode for repeating patterns.
- 3D layers that bring models and shapes into a 2D canvas.

Palettes can be picked from presets, imported or made inside the app. Work exports as PNG, animated PNG, spritesheets, GIF or video, and a command-line interface handles bulk exports. You can attach custom data to layers, frames and cels for a game pipeline, and add community extensions. Pixelorama also keeps automatic backups for recovery after a crash.

The app is built with the Godot engine. The Mac download from GitHub releases is a DMG holding a Universal app for Apple silicon and Intel, and itch.io also offers a Mac version.

Aseprite, a paid editor with a trial version, also has a tiled mode, onion skin and a command-line interface. Pixelorama is free under the MIT license.
