Koodo Reader keeps a library of ebooks and documents and opens them in a reader with highlights, notes and bookmarks. It reads EPUB, PDF, DRM-free MOBI and AZW3, plain text, FictionBook, comic archives, DjVu, Markdown and Word files, among others.

- Pages show as one column, two columns or a continuous scroll, with your choice of font, spacing, colors and margins.
- Text-to-speech, translation and dictionary lookup work inside the book, including local MDX dictionaries.
- Notes and highlights export as CSV, Markdown, HTML, TXT or PDF, and can be sent to Readwise, Notion, Obsidian or Joplin.
- Reading progress can sync with KOReader, and the library can be shared as an OPDS feed.

The site's FAQ says Koodo Reader Pro keeps books, reading progress, bookmarks, highlights and notes in step across your devices. Pro does this through a cloud storage service, Docker, WebDAV, FTP, SFTP, SMB or object storage. On the pricing page, the Free version has no book limit in the web and desktop apps, while the Android and iOS apps hold 20 books without Pro.

On GitHub, the Mac build comes as separate DMGs for Apple silicon and Intel, and Homebrew has a cask. The same app runs on Windows, Linux, Android, iOS and the web.

The AI translation, dictionary, summary and encyclopedia tools run on a model you configure. A password, a PIN or Touch ID can lock the library. The README says the app includes no tracking services and never uploads your reading data on its own.
