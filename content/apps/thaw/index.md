Thaw splits your menu bar into a visible section and a hidden one. You keep the items you check often in view, and the rest wait until you click an empty part of the bar, hover, scroll or press a shortcut. A keyboard search finds any item, hidden ones included, and opens it.

- Groups move and hide several items together, and a group can sit in the bar as a folder with its own icon.
- Profiles save a whole layout. You can switch them by hand or have Thaw switch when you change display, Space or Focus.
- Zen Mode hides every section with one shortcut and can turn on by itself while you share your screen.
- A layout editor shows every item in one window, and you can arrange them from the keyboard.

On a MacBook with a notch, the Thaw Bar puts the items the notch would cover in a separate bar just under the menu bar. Each action has a `thaw://` URL, which lets Shortcuts, Raycast or a shell script drive it.

Thaw needs the Accessibility permission to move items at all. Screen Recording is optional and adds live previews and a wallpaper tint. The developer says Thaw has no analytics and goes online only for updates and their release notes, both of which you can turn off.

Which version you get depends on your Mac. The current 2.1.0 release on GitHub is a Universal app for macOS 26, notarized by Apple. Thaw 1 covers macOS 14, and betas cover macOS 15 and 27.

Bartender also hides and searches menu bar items, and it is sold after a trial. Thaw is free under GPL-3.0 and asks for no account.
