JASP runs statistical analyses without asking you to write code. You open a data set in a spreadsheet layout, pick an analysis such as a t-test or a regression, drag variables into place and set its options, and JASP produces the tables and plots. Change a setting and the results update.

The project aims to cover both frequentist and Bayesian statistics, and many analyses come in both forms. The modules go well beyond the basics:

- t-tests, ANOVA, mixed models and regression, classical and Bayesian
- factor analysis, structural equation modeling and network analysis
- meta-analysis, including Cochrane meta-analyses
- machine learning, time series, survival and quality control

Tables and graphs support APA format, so you can copy them straight into Word. JASP opens CSV and other text files, Excel and OpenDocument spreadsheets, and SPSS, SAS, Stata, R and Minitab data files, and it is integrated with the Open Science Framework. The developers say JASP never collects user-identifiable information, and the download page states that no internet connection is needed to run it.

The project wants JASP to feel familiar to SPSS users. Behind the interface, a separate engine process runs the analyses, which are written in R. The name stands for Jeffreys's Amazing Statistics Program, after the Bayesian statistician Harold Jeffreys.

The Mac version needs macOS 15 Sequoia or later, with separate downloads for Apple silicon and Intel. The project asks for about 4 GB of disk space and recommends at least 8 GB of memory. The University of Amsterdam supports the project.
