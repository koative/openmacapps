Anki quizzes you with flashcards and decides when each one comes back. After you see the answer, you rate how well you remembered it. Anki then schedules the next review for around the time you would otherwise forget. Cards you know well turn up less and less, which leaves more of each session for the hard ones.

Cards are grouped in decks, so you can drill one subject without the rest of your collection. A card can hold text, images, audio, video and scientific markup. You can change both how cards look and how the review timing works, and collections of more than 100,000 cards stay usable.

Add-ons extend the desktop app with new features. Shared decks let you start from material other people have made, and you can publish your own.

The free AnkiWeb service syncs your cards between devices. On phones, AnkiMobile is the official iOS app and is sold to fund development, while AnkiDroid on Android costs nothing and is made by contributors.

Version 26.09.4 needs macOS 13 or later. It comes as two DMGs, one for Apple silicon and one for Intel. The code is licensed under the AGPL, version 3 or later, and Anki is a registered trademark of Ankitects.
