Textream puts your script at the top of the Mac screen and moves through it as you talk. Paste the text, press play and start speaking. When you reach the end, the overlay closes by itself.

Three modes set the pace. Word Tracking highlights each word as speech recognition hears it. Classic scrolls at a steady 0.5 to 8 words a second. Voice-Activated scrolls only while you speak and stops when you pause.

- The prompt can hang from the notch, float in a window that follows your cursor, or fill a screen.
- It can mirror onto an external display or a Sidecar iPad, flipped for teleprompter glass.
- A setting keeps the overlay out of screen recordings and video calls.
- Dropping in a PowerPoint file turns its presenter notes into pages.
- A phone or another computer on your network can show the prompt in a browser, and a director can edit and push the script live.

The developer says there are no accounts, ads or analytics. Word Tracking uses macOS speech recognition, which may send your microphone audio to Apple.

Version 1.7.1 has two universal disk images, one for macOS 26 and one for macOS 15, and the app is notarized. The same App Store listing includes an iPhone and iPad companion that records camera video while you read, without the prompt in the saved clip.
