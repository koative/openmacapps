Tropy is for the photos you take in an archive: hundreds of shots of letters, ledgers and pages that land on your disk as IMG_5261.JPG. You import them into a project, combine the photos of one document into a single item, and describe it with metadata from a template you can adjust.

- Tags and lists group items however your project needs, whatever order the archive filed them in.
- Notes and transcriptions sit with the item, and you can select a detail inside a photo.
- Image adjustments help make a faded page easier to read.
- Search runs across items, metadata, notes and tags.
- Projects export as JSON-LD, CSV or a ZIP archive. Items can go to Zotero or Omeka S, and IIIF manifests can be imported.

Project data is kept in SQLite, the storage format the Library of Congress recommends for preservation. CSV, ZIP, Zotero, Omeka S and IIIF support come as core plugins, and the plugin specification lets you write your own export plugin for a format Tropy lacks.

Tropy started out at George Mason University's Roy Rosenzweig Center for History and New Media (RRCHNM). It is now developed jointly with the Luxembourg Centre for Contemporary and Digital History and Digital Scholar, the nonprofit that also runs Zotero and Omeka.

Version 1.17.3 has a separate Apple silicon DMG, and the app inside is notarized. Tropy also runs on Windows and Linux.
