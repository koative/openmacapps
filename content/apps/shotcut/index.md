Shotcut edits video on a multitrack timeline. It works on files where they are, with no import step, so clips of different formats, resolutions and frame rates can sit together in one project. FFmpeg does the decoding, so current audio and video formats open, along with image sequences and images such as AVIF, WebP and SVG.

- Video filters run from chroma key, stabilization and motion tracking to 3D LUTs, lens correction and three-way color wheels.
- Audio gets loudness and peak scopes, noise reduction, automatic ducking, voiceover recorded straight to the timeline and surround or Ambisonic layouts.
- Subtitles can be imported from SRT, VTT, ASS and SSA, generated from speech, edited and burned in.
- Projects handle 4K and 8K, HDR10 and HLG, and 10-bit video from input to export.

Export also covers single frames, image sequences, CMX3600 edit decision lists and batch conversion. The app can capture from a webcam and play network streams.

Final Cut Pro is Apple's editor for Mac and iPad, sold as a one-time App Store purchase or inside the Apple Creator Studio bundle. Shotcut is free and open source, and the same editor runs on Windows and Linux.

Older Macs get their own builds. The current one is a Universal disk image for macOS 12 and later. Version 24.11.17 is the last for macOS 11, and 24.01.31 the last for 10.14 and 10.15. Macs from 2013 or earlier can show wrong preview colors since the switch to Metal, and 22.12.21 is offered for them. An unsigned app bundle is published too, for anyone who wants to modify the build under the GPL.
