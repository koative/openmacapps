Shotnix takes screenshots and screen recordings from the menu bar, then lets you mark up a shot or turn a recording into a finished demo video. Area, window and full-screen shots have default shortcuts, Shift-Command-4, 5 and 3.

- Captures take a region, one window, the whole screen, every connected display at once as separate images, or a scrolling page joined into one tall image.
- The markup editor draws arrows, boxes, ellipses, text and callout bubbles, numbers steps, dims everything outside a spotlight, and blurs or pixelates private details.
- Text recognition copies text from any part of the screen and keeps tables in reading order. History finds old captures by the text inside them.
- Shots can be pinned to float on the desktop, where they can be moved and resized.

Recordings run at 60 fps and can include system audio, the microphone and a camera. A crash does not lose the take. Each recording opens in the video editor with zooms on your clicks, a smoothed redraw of the real cursor and your saved look already applied. Captions and edit-by-text come from your narration through Apple's on-device speech recognition, and macOS 15 adds caption translation. Exports go to MP4 or GIF.

CleanShot X covers similar captures, and its Basic plan is a one-time purchase with a year of updates. It also uploads captures to CleanShot Cloud and hands back a share link. Shotnix has no account, no cloud and no share links, and it is free under the MIT license. The developer says the app goes online only to check for updates.

Shotnix needs macOS 13 or later on an Apple silicon Mac; the official download does not run on Intel. The v0.30.0-beta DMG is notarized. Current releases still carry a beta tag.
