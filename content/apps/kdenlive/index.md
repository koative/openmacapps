Kdenlive puts your clips, music and titles on a multitrack timeline where you trim, rearrange and layer them, then renders the result to a video file. The name stands for KDE Non-Linear Video Editor, and the KDE community builds it on the MLT multimedia framework.

- Footage in almost any audio or video format goes into bins with tags and filters, and proxy clips keep playback smooth on slower machines.
- Editing covers multicam, three-point edits, nested sequences and markers, with automatic backups of the project.
- Hundreds of audio and video effects accept keyframes, and compositions combine layers.
- Color work runs from LUTs to detailed grading, checked on a histogram, vectorscope, RGB parade and waveform.
- Subtitles can start from automatic transcription, and the audio side has mixers and voiceover recording.

Final Cut Pro is Apple's own editor, sold once on the App Store or as part of a subscription bundle. Kdenlive is free and open source, and it also runs on Linux, Windows and BSD.

Version 26.08.3 needs macOS 13 Ventura or newer. Separate disk images exist for Apple silicon and Intel, and the Apple silicon image is signed by KDE e.V. and notarized. Daily builds are also offered for testing. They can corrupt existing projects, so keep them away from work you care about.
