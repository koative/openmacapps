ScreenHint turns part of your screen into a floating picture that stays above other windows. Press your shortcut, select a region, and the capture becomes a hint you can keep next to your work: notes you refer to, two versions of your work to compare, or a reminder you want in sight.

- Hints can be resized, thrown around the desktop and pinned to desktops.
- Copying a hint puts the image on the clipboard, so it pastes anywhere images go.
- Copy Text runs text recognition on the hint. If the first pass finds nothing, it tries again and tells you when there is no text.
- The shortcuts listed in a hint's menu, such as Command-C, work from the keyboard.

The first-run tour shows a demo video, asks for the two permissions the app needs and lets you set the shortcut. Those permissions are Screen Recording and Accessibility, and a Permissions window shows whether each one is granted and opens the right pane of System Settings.

ScreenHint needs macOS 14 or later. The 1.6.0 release on GitHub is a ZIP holding a Universal app for Apple silicon and Intel, Developer ID-signed and notarized. The developer also publishes it on the Mac App Store and runs a beta program on TestFlight. The code is GPL-3.0.

The developer says the screenshot behind a hint is used only to display it and is never stored or sent anywhere.
