Super Productivity keeps your to-do list and your working day in one place. You break tasks into sub-tasks, sort them into projects and tags, then drag them onto a schedule timeline that sits next to your calendar events. You can timebox each task and track the time you spend on it.

- Boards sort tasks visually, with a Kanban board and an Eisenhower Matrix built in, or layouts of your own.
- Focus mode runs Pomodoro, Flowtime or countdown sessions, and a break reminder tells you when to step away.
- Tracked time turns into time sheets and work summaries you can export to a company time-tracking system.
- Tasks assigned to you can be imported from Jira, GitHub, GitLab, Linear, Azure DevOps, Trello and several other trackers, with work logs created automatically.
- Notes, file attachments and project bookmarks keep context next to the work.

CalDAV support is basic. Plugins and custom themes can extend the app and change how it looks.

The app is offline-first and needs no account or registration. Your data stays where you put it. You can sync it between devices through Dropbox, WebDAV or SuperSync, the project's own end-to-end encrypted service, which you can also host yourself. The developer says the app collects no data.

Todoist also keeps tasks and projects in one place on every device. Super Productivity is MIT-licensed and adds time tracking and focus sessions to the list.

The Mac version comes from GitHub releases as Apple silicon, Intel and Universal DMGs, and from the App Store. The Apple silicon build requires macOS 13 and is Developer ID-signed and notarized. The same app runs on Windows, Linux, Android, iOS and in a browser.
