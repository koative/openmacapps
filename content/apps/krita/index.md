You paint in Krita by picking one of the more than 100 bundled brush presets and drawing on the canvas, usually with a graphics tablet. Any tablet that works with your operating system is supported.

- Brushes come from more than nine engines, among them Color Smudge, Shape, Particle and a filter engine, and you can save and tag your own.
- Three stabilizer methods smooth out shaky strokes.
- Drawing assistants guide straight lines, ellipses, vanishing points and fisheye perspective.
- Vector tools and a library of speech-bubble templates help with comic panels, stored as SVG.
- Wrap-around mode repeats the image along both axes so you can paint textures that tile without visible joins.

Switching to the animation workspace adds a timeline with onion skinning, simple tweening of opacity and position, and imported audio. Finished animations export as video or as a sequence of images. Color management uses LCMS for ICC profiles and OpenColorIO for EXR, and Python scripts can add your own dockers and plugins.

Krita opens and saves PSD files, so artwork can move between it and Adobe Photoshop. Krita itself has no trial and no subscription.

The download page lists macOS 10.15, at least 4 GB of memory with 16 GB recommended, and OpenGL 3.0. The free macOS installer is a DMG from krita.org. A paid Mac App Store version updates automatically and funds development. Krita does not run on iPad or iPhone.
