Mixxx puts up to four decks on screen for mixing digital music files live. You drop a track onto a deck and it starts playing, with a scrolling waveform for beats and cue points and an overview of the whole song for seeking.

- BPM and musical key detection run over your library, and Sync Lock keeps up to four decks matched in tempo and beat.
- Keylock changes tempo without changing pitch. Hotcues, quantized loops of 4, 8 or 16 beats and beat rolls let you rework a track while it plays.
- Effect chains link up to three effects, and up to 64 sampler decks layer sounds over the mix.
- Auto DJ takes over with automatic crossfades when you step away.
- Mixes can be recorded to WAV, FLAC or Ogg Vorbis, or streamed live to a Shoutcast or Icecast server.

Mixxx comes with presets for many MIDI and HID controllers, and you can write your own mappings in JavaScript. Timecode vinyl or CDs on turntables or CDJs can drive the decks too. The library holds crates and playlists, looks up missing tags on MusicBrainz, and reads existing iTunes, Traktor, Rekordbox and Serato libraries. It plays FLAC, WAV, AIFF, MP3, AAC, Ogg Vorbis and Opus files.

Mixxx 2.5.6 needs macOS 11 or later. The download page offers separate DMGs for Apple silicon and Intel, and the Apple silicon disk image is notarized. The project says there is no paid or pro edition.
