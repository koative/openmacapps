You browse in Firefox, Mozilla's web browser, and shape it with extensions, themes and tab tools while it blocks trackers on its own. Tracker blocking is on from the first launch, with nothing to set up.

- Tab groups, pinned tabs, Firefox View and a customizable sidebar keep a crowded session in order.
- Reader mode clears clutter from articles, and picture-in-picture keeps a video in view while you work elsewhere.
- PDFs open in the browser and can be edited there.
- Mozilla lists more than 30,000 extensions and thousands of light and dark themes.
- When you switch, Firefox brings over your bookmarks, passwords and history from the old browser.

Firefox also runs on phones and tablets, so tabs, history and passwords can follow you between devices. Its AI features are optional, and you can turn them off. Mozilla says it does not sell your personal data.

Firefox needs macOS 10.15 or later on an Intel or Apple silicon Mac. Older Macs are pointed to the Extended Support Release instead. The download page also offers Beta, Developer Edition and Nightly builds in more than 90 languages.

Vivaldi is built on Chromium's open-source code, and its own help pages say it is not released under one unified open-source license. Firefox's source code is published under the Mozilla Public License 2.0, though the Firefox name and logo stay Mozilla trademarks.
