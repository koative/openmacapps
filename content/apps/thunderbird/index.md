Thunderbird puts your email, calendars and contacts in one desktop program. Add your mail accounts, then read each one on its own or all of them together in a unified inbox.

- Filters and tags organize your messages the way you set them up.
- A calendar and an address book live in the same app as your mail.
- Microsoft Exchange accounts connect through Exchange Web Services, supported since Thunderbird 145.
- Add-ons extend the program, though a given release may not work with every add-on.

Thunderbird ships on four channels, and two of them are meant for everyday use. The standard Release adds features and fixes every month. The Extended Support Release collects new features once a year and ships stability and security fixes monthly. Beta and Daily are for testing.

Thunderbird 159 needs macOS 10.15 or later and runs on Intel and Apple silicon Macs. It is built on the Mozilla platform that Firefox uses, and its code goes into a subdirectory of the Firefox source to be built. Versions for Windows and Linux and an Android app come from the same project.

Microsoft Outlook for Mac also reads Gmail, iCloud, Yahoo and IMAP accounts without charge, but if you have no Microsoft 365 subscription it shows ads in your inbox. Thunderbird is free and paid for by donations. Its developers say it collects no personal data and sells no ads in your inbox.
