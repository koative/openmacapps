Stellarium draws the sky for a place and time you choose, in 3D, as it looks to the naked eye, through binoculars or through a telescope. It has time control, a strong zoom, coordinate grids, and constellation artwork and labels that can be shown over the sky.

- The default catalogues hold more than 600,000 stars and 80,000 deep-sky objects. Optional catalogues add over 220 million stars and more than a million deep-sky objects.
- Constellations come from more than 40 sky cultures, and calendars from over 35.
- The simulation includes the atmosphere, sunrise and sunset, eclipses, comet tails, novae and supernovae.
- Plugins add artificial satellites, an ocular view and control of a real telescope.

For planetarium domes there is fisheye projection, and a spheric-mirror projection serves low-cost home-built domes. A scripting interface and an HTTP interface for remote control are built in. You can add your own landscapes, deep-sky objects, constellation images and scripts.

The current Mac download is a Universal Qt6 build for macOS 11 or later. The recommended setup is 4 GiB of RAM, a graphics card with OpenGL 3.3 and 10 GiB of disk space. A PDF user guide is published alongside release 26.3. Stellarium is released under GPL-2.0 or later.
