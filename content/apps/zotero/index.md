Zotero catches a source while you browse and files it in a library you can search, annotate and cite from. With the Zotero Connector installed, one click saves an article from JSTOR, a preprint from arXiv, a news story or a book from a library catalog. The Safari connector ships inside the app; you switch it on in Safari's Extensions settings.

In the library you sort items into collections and tag them. Saved searches fill themselves as matching items arrive. PDFs open in Zotero's own reader, where you add annotations and notes.

- Citations and bibliographies in more than 9,000 styles.
- Plugins for Word and LibreOffice, and support for Google Docs, that insert citations as you write.
- Optional sync of items, notes and files across devices, with your library also reachable from a web browser.
- Group libraries shared with as many people as you like, free of charge.

File sync goes through Zotero Storage. A small allowance is free, and more space is a yearly subscription. Group libraries draw on the group owner's storage at no extra charge.

Zotero needs macOS 10.15 or later. The Word plugin works with Word 2016 to 2024 or Office 365 on the Mac, and the LibreOffice plugin needs a Java Development Kit on macOS. Mobile apps exist for iOS and Android.

The nonprofit Digital Scholar develops Zotero with outside contributors, and the code is licensed under the AGPL, version 3 or later.
