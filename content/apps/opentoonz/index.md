OpenToonz takes 2D animation from drawings to finished footage. You draw or bring in artwork, time it on an exposure sheet or a timeline, paint it, and composite the shots with effects.

The software grew out of Toonz, made by Digital Video in Rome. Studio Ghibli customised its own version over many years for ink and paint, colour design and compositing, and Dwango published that code as OpenToonz.

- Both raster and vector drawings, with anti-aliased lines.
- Painting data is kept apart from the palette, so you can fill with provisional colours and change them later.
- Effects are wired together in a node tree, and a plug-in SDK lets anyone add new ones.
- Images keep their real-world scale, set by resolution in dpi and units such as millimetres.
- Coloured, binarised TGA sequences convert to and from the OpenToonz format, for studios with an existing pipeline.

Version 1.8 reworked many drawing tools, including the assistant, brush, fill and tape tools. The GTS scanning tool from Studio Ghibli runs on Windows only.

On the Mac, the 1.8 installer is a PKG holding an Intel (x86_64) app. The site lists macOS 10.14 Mojave or later, 4 GB of RAM and a 1280 by 1024 display. Nightly builds and older versions are on the releases page. The project warns that its only official sites are opentoonz.github.io and the GitHub repository.

Moho approaches 2D animation through bone rigging, with inverse kinematics and physics for posing characters, and is sold under a perpetual license. OpenToonz centres on the exposure sheet and compositing, under the BSD license, free for commercial and non-commercial work.
